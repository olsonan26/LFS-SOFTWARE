# Person Profile Report Skill

**Skill version:** `lfs-person-profile-v1.0`

## Purpose

Turn the analyst-reviewed checkbox selections from the six Lettrology core perspectives into a polished, coherent profile report. The report must synthesize the checked traits into a readable whole rather than simply repeating lists.

## Source hierarchy

1. The analyst's checked traits are the authoritative behavioral inputs.
2. The six perspective labels explain where each selected trait belongs in the Lettrology framework.
3. Unchecked traits are not claims about the person and must not appear as inferred characteristics.
4. The person's case role is context only. It must never be used to infer guilt, innocence, victim behavior, dangerousness, deception, motive, or criminal propensity.
5. Do not introduce outside biography, diagnoses, events, or facts that were not supplied in the request.

## Six perspectives

- **Initial Impressions** — how the person may initially present or be perceived.
- **Personality Description** — broader personality expression across situations.
- **Heart's Desire** — internal desires, values, motivations, and emotional pull.
- **Habits & Tendencies** — recurring or automatic patterns, especially under pressure.
- **Natural Skills & Talents** — abilities and strengths that may come more naturally.
- **Ultimate Goal** — longer-term direction, aims, or the kind of outcome toward which the person may gravitate.

## Writing standard

Write like a highly experienced profile writer preparing a serious professional report for investigators, researchers, and practitioners. The prose should be nuanced, specific, natural, and psychologically literate without pretending to be a clinical assessment.

The finished report should:

- read as one integrated portrait rather than six disconnected mini-readings;
- explain how elevated and shadow traits can coexist in the same person;
- identify tensions and contradictions instead of smoothing them over;
- notice genuinely recurring checked themes across multiple perspectives;
- explain how the same trait can express differently depending on the perspective in which it appears;
- distinguish outward presentation from inward motivation, habits, skills, and long-term direction;
- use careful language such as “may,” “can,” “tends to,” “is likely to present as,” and “the selected traits suggest” where appropriate;
- avoid repetitive phrasing, filler, vague inspirational language, and list-dumping;
- remain readable to a non-specialist while still feeling sophisticated and analytical;
- normally fall between roughly 1,200 and 2,000 words unless the selected material is too sparse to justify that length.

## Forensic and criminology guardrails

This software may be used with suspects, victims, witnesses, persons of interest, associates, or reference subjects. Therefore:

- Never state or imply that a Lettrology trait proves criminal behavior, guilt, innocence, truthfulness, violence, dangerousness, or future offending.
- Never diagnose a mental disorder or infer a health condition.
- Never blame a victim or suggest that personality traits caused victimization.
- Never convert a shadow trait into an allegation of misconduct.
- If a checked trait itself concerns manipulation, deception, control, aggression, jealousy, rule resistance, or similar behavior, discuss it as an analyst-selected interpretive tendency, not as evidence that a specific act occurred.
- Separate interpretive observations from factual evidence. The report is a Lettrology-based profile, not a forensic finding or psychological diagnosis.
- Do not sensationalize.

## Integration rules

When several checked traits point in the same direction, describe this as a recurring selected theme and identify the perspectives in which it appears. Do not call it statistically significant or validated evidence.

When elevated and shadow traits appear together, explain the possible range of expression. A person may be dependable in some contexts and controlling in others, independent yet sensitive to rejection, generous yet resentful under strain, or highly adaptable while also avoiding commitment. Treat these combinations as context-dependent patterns rather than contradictions that must be resolved.

If a perspective has no checked traits on one side, do not invent them. If both sides are empty but the analyst marked the section reviewed, state only that no traits from that expression were selected for that perspective.

## Required report structure

### Title
A professional title specific to the person, without sensational language.

### Executive Summary
A strong 2–4 paragraph integrated overview. Lead with the clearest recurring themes and the central tension between strengths and shadow expressions. Do not simply summarize each section in order.

### Integrated Profile Sections
Return 6–8 substantive sections. Use descriptive headings appropriate to the actual selections. Across the sections, cover:

- outward presentation and social impression;
- broader personality pattern;
- internal motivations and emotional drivers;
- habitual responses and stress tendencies;
- natural abilities and how they may be used constructively or poorly;
- longer-term direction and goals;
- cross-perspective tensions or recurring themes;
- neutral investigative/contextual considerations when relevant.

Each section should normally contain 2–4 developed paragraphs.

### Analyst Summary
Provide 5–8 concise takeaways that are directly grounded in the checked traits. These are not risk scores, diagnoses, or conclusions about criminal behavior.

### Interpretive Note
Clearly state that the report synthesizes analyst-selected Lettrology traits; it is not a clinical psychological assessment, a determination of truthfulness, evidence of guilt, or a prediction of criminal behavior.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not:

- reproduce the checkbox lists as the report;
- invent unselected traits;
- infer hidden motives from the person's case role;
- write a “good person / bad person” verdict;
- assign a criminality score, danger score, psychopathy label, or diagnosis;
- state that any trait proves a crime, lie, intent, or future act;
- use generic numerology material that was not supplied in the request;
- mention model reasoning, hidden chain-of-thought, or internal deliberation.
